HMWK 3 — Stochastic Penetration Models & Limit Theorems

Modeling System Vulnerabilities using Bernoulli & Rademacher Processes

1. Stochastic Modeling Framework ($n$ Systems & $m$ Attackers)

To analyze the dynamics of cybersecurity penetration processes across multiple systems and attackers, we model the cumulative score using independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random increments \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\).

  • Bernoulli Model: Increments represent success/failure at each step, where \(X_i \in \{0, 1\}\) with probability \(\Pr(X_i = 1) = p\) and \(\Pr(X_i = 0) = 1 - p\).
  • Rademacher Model: Introduced to study the symmetric, zero-mean case, where \(X_i \in \{-1, +1\}\) with equal probability \(\Pr(X_i = +1) = \Pr(X_i = -1) = 1/2\).

3. Quantities of Interest & Statistical Analysis

We analyze the distribution and asymptotic behavior of three key aggregate metrics as the number of steps \(n\) increases:

Quantity Mathematical Expression Asymptotic Behavior & Statistical Meaning
Total Score \(S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i\) Grows linearly with \(n\); variance scales as \(\mathcal{O}(n)\). Reflects cumulative penetration depth.
Average Score \(\frac{S_n}{n} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i\) Converges in probability to the expected value \(\mathbb{E}[X_1]\) according to the Law of Large Numbers (LLN).
Normalized Total Score \(\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i\) Converges in distribution to a normal distribution \(\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)\) according to the Central Limit Theorem (CLT).
Law of Large Numbers: \(\lim_{n \to \infty} \Pr\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n} - \mu\right| > \varepsilon\right) = 0 \quad (\text{where } \mu = \mathbb{E}[X])\)
Central Limit Theorem (Rademacher Case): \(\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty\)

4. Interpretation in the Context of Systems and Attackers

  • System Size (\(n\)): Represents the depth or sequence of security barriers. As \(n\) increases, individual anomalies smooth out, and aggregate behavior becomes highly predictable due to LLN.
  • Number of Attackers (\(m\)): Represents parallel sample paths. Running multiple trajectories illustrates the spread of outcomes around the expected mean.
  • Variance & Normalization: Without division by \(\sqrt{n}\), the variance of \(S_n\) diverges as \(n\) grows. Normalizing by \(\sqrt{n}\) stabilizes the scale, allowing direct observation of bell-curve convergence via the CLT.
