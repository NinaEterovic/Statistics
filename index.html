HMWK 1 — Public-Key Cryptography

Elliptic-Curve Scalar Multiplication over Finite Field \(\mathbb{Z}_{17}\)

1. Theoretical Background

Elliptic-curve scalar multiplication serves as a fundamental cryptographic one-way function. It is efficient to compute in the forward direction (\(k \to Q = kG\)), while reversing it—known as the Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem (ECDLP)—is computationally intractable for large prime fields.

2. Toy Curve Parameters

  • Curve Equation: \(y^2 = x^3 + 7 \pmod{17}\)
  • Base Generator Point (\(G\)): \((1, 5)\)
  • Private Key (\(k\)): 6

4. Statistical Exploration & Findings

  • Coordinate Distribution: The resulting sequence of coordinates spreads pseudo-randomly across the finite field \(\mathbb{Z}_{17}\).
  • Avalanche Behavior: Modifying the private key \(k\) shifts the resulting point drastically across the curve due to the non-linear algebraic group laws.
  • Real-world Relevance: Ethereum and Bitcoin utilize the exact same algebraic principles via the standardized \(\text{secp256k1}\) curve over a 256-bit prime field.
