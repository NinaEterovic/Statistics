Numerically Stable Statistics & Empirical Validation of Means and Variances

Ex. 1 – Welford’s Recurrence Formulas

Following Knuth's approach for the recursive mean, we derive Welford’s formulas to compute the mean \(\bar{x}_n\), sum of squares (\(M_{2,n}\)), and sample variance \(s^2_n\) in a single pass with high numerical stability, avoiding catastrophic cancellation.

Derivation Outline

  • Mean update: \(\bar{x}_n = \bar{x}_{n-1} + \frac{x_n - \bar{x}_{n-1}}{n}\)
  • Sum of Squares ($M_2$): \(M_{2,n} = M_{2,n-1} + (x_n - \bar{x}_{n-1})(x_n - \bar{x}_n)\)
  • Sample Variance: \(s^2_n = \frac{M_{2,n}}{n-1}\) (or population variance \(\sigma^2_n = \frac{M_{2,n}}{n}\))

Core JavaScript Implementation (3-line logic)

let mean = 0, M2 = 0;
data.forEach((x, i) => {
    let delta = x - mean;
    mean += delta / (i + 1);
    M2 += delta * (x - mean);
});

Ex. 2 – Sampling Mean and Variance in Penetration Testing

Each penetration attempt on a system yields a score between 0 and 10, drawn from a uniform distribution over \([0, 10]\). For this distribution:

  • Theoretical Mean (\(\mu\)): \(5.0\)
  • Theoretical Variance (\(\sigma^2\)): \(\frac{(b-a)^2}{12} = \frac{10^2}{12} \approx 8.333\)

We simulate \(m\) hackers attempting \(n\) systems. Using Welford's algorithm, we compute sampling means and variances for each hacker, then average them across all \(m\) hackers to compare with theoretical benchmarks.

3. Conclusions & Statistical Interpretation

  • Unbiased Estimation: The average of sampling means across all hackers closely approximates the theoretical mean \(\mu\), and the average of sampling variances matches \(\sigma^2\). This confirms that sample statistics are unbiased estimators of population parameters.
  • Effect of Increasing \(n\): According to the Law of Large Numbers and properties of variance (\(\text{Var}(\bar{X}) = \sigma^2 / n\)), increasing the number of systems \(n\) drastically reduces the variability (spread) of sampling means around \(\mu\).
  • Usefulness in Inferential Statistics: These recurrence formulas allow efficient, online updating of statistical parameters in real-time security telemetry and streaming data analysis without storing historical datasets.
